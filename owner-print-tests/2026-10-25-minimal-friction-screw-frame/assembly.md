# Assembly and physical comparison

## A — one motor / one pin

Use **variant L**, gear approximately 2.0–3.25 mm above the metal motor top. K gives only about 0.5 mm minimum straight engagement at full float and is **not released for powered testing**. No need to remeasure the already recorded motor dimensions. The real C fit, tab/glue variation and absence of shaft motion still require observation.

1. Keep the screw and towers upright during cleanup. With flush cutters, support the screw locally and cut the **four thin necks**, alternating sides; do not twist/pull a tower. Remove the brim. Look for cracks and raised nubs on each external thread contact. Do not shave the flange, washer seat or C ribs. If a nub catches the pin, stop rather than force it through.
2. Reuse the already cleaned 40–50 mm pin: 4.4 square, short lower internal thread, open upper square bore. The wide old sacrificial foot must already be absent; it is not a functional stop in this design. A pin retaining that 12 mm foot does not fit the intended travel. The supplied optional clean pin mesh has no such foot. Do not cut off any functional guide or stop.
3. First fit the pin onto the bare screw, without the motor/frame. Check several screw positions through 15 mm of rise. The original profile/lead is retained, but tower removal and printing can still spoil it. Use fingertips only.
4. Put the washer flat in the lower pocket. Seat the rotor flange on it. Before installing the motor, close the halves and gently alternate downward/upward rotor reaction: washer/deck below and captured flange/stop above must react the load. Nominal rotor lift is **0.30 mm**. Both thrust surfaces must permit rotation. This also separates frame drag from motor/C-fit drag.
5. Open again; seat the L metal body at Z2, PCB to the open **−X side**, wires outside the ring/rotor at X≤−4.4. Retain the tested shim choice; use a thinner existing shim if the halves cannot seat. Do not squeeze the PCB, glue or solder. No new spring bridge is present.
6. Lower the C socket over the gear without forcing. Confirm rotor-to-washer seating and small axial float remain possible **with** the motor. Watch that the gear/shaft does not move with the rotor. If the C fit sticks axially, the shaft can carry frictional thrust despite an open cavity roof: stop.
7. Both new halves belong together. Turn the back over by rotation, not reflection: its motor opening and PCB channel align with the front, and all three pegs meet their pockets. Use existing bands around the grooves near Z7, 36 and 56. Keep wires outside the upper bands. Close until the hard split faces meet; do not crush the frame.
8. Put the pin in the two short square guide stations. Low pin bottom is **Z19.5** with rotor seated; the thread extends Z16.5–43.5. For a 50 mm pin the tip is Z69.5, **9.5 mm above the 60 mm frame**; for a 40 mm pin it is Z59.5. Mark the initial tip position with a removable paper reference. Thread travel +3/+7.5/+15 mm gives corresponding tip rise; float can add 0.30 mm.
9. Turn the exposed screw just below the pin gently with fingertips. Check each direction at low, +3, +7.5, +15; five manual return cycles are sufficient for this comparison. Keep fingers from pushing the pin sideways. Record whether motion changes when the bands are briefly loosened; restore normal closure before powered testing.
10. Only after manual acceptance, run a few short slow cycles with your existing hardware/software and known actual settings. Start with 3 mm. Do not use time alone as proof of travel; observe the reference mark. Maximum authorized prototype range here is **15 mm**, not 40 mm. Disconnect at any obstruction; do not raise IRUN.

There is no safe crash stop at +15. Thread geometry permits further motion, but the lower guide begins losing overlap beyond about +16.2 mm at maximum float. Stay inside the marked range. The minimum 6 mm engagement retains **3 mm nominal thread-end margin** at +15. No load rating, speed or durability is established.

## B — 3×3 neighbors at 5.08 mm pitch

The nine identical manual columns are deliberately **not screw driven**. Their upper 30 mm is 4.4 mm square; round Ø2.4 stems permit rotation and independent translation. They test exterior contact. They do not test screw self-locking or an entire unsupported array.

1. Turn the stand over: its flat deck is now at Z20–22 and its four legs rest on the table. Insert all nine stems. Identify the center with a marker. The center stem hole is Ø4.8, giving 1.2 mm radial clearance and no antirotation; neighboring holes Ø3.6 give nominal 0.6 mm radial play. The holes do **not** intersect.
2. Put one short spacer around each outer stem below its square body. Because spacers contact the tapered transition, the nominal low square-body bottom is **Z27.25**, not Z25. This is an assembly calculation, not a precision gauge. The center is held manually at a matching level. All nine top faces should initially be level.
3. For the **fixed-neighbor geometry condition**, lightly hold the chosen neighbors in place. Raise/lower and twist the center without forcing. This condition only tests geometry; fixed neighbors exaggerate restraint compared with a real moving array.
4. Add a tall 15 mm spacer beneath the short spacer on four alternate outer stems. Their square bodies rise 15 mm, leaving 15 mm mutual overlap. Test center travel and twist again. Remove a cardinal neighbor, then a corner neighbor, to expose boundary/gap behavior. Record which change matters.
5. Lift all pins out to add/remove the optional Ø3.0-hole liner. Its top raises the datum **1.2 mm**, but the imposed height difference remains 15. Neighbor stem radial play becomes 0.3 mm; the center opening remains Ø4.8. Check stems slide before comparing body friction. The loose fixture intentionally permits grid errors that may consume the 0.68 mm face gaps.
6. The square boundary ring stands on four legs on the deck, opening **15.0 ×15.0**, ring Z32–34. With all outer pins low, nominal outer face clearance is 0.22 mm. The ring limits array spreading. Raised neighbors can leave this ring entirely: that loss is an intended test condition, not hidden full-height support. Test ring on and off.
7. For **movable neighbors**, first leave them unheld upright on their spacers and observe whether a rising center lifts one. Then remove spacers, lay the stand on its side, position the bodies around their low positions and slide the center ± a small distance while every neighbor can move freely in either axial direction. Keep all stems through the deck and the square bodies off the table. Test with/without boundary, then stagger bodies by 15 mm. This horizontal fixture condition removes gravitational height holding; report it separately from the upright test. Touching/falling pins or stem/deck rubbing invalidate a neighbor-only friction conclusion.

No replacement pins of different width are mixed in. Both play settings retain 5.08 mm *nominal centers*; the round clearance holes intentionally allow departures from those centers. Pin-to-pin overlap disappears at a 30 mm height difference for these 30 mm bodies; a real 50 mm pin would lose it at 50 mm difference, before considering lower fixed structures.

## C — same pin geometry, different print orientation

Two 18 mm upright and two identical 18 mm horizontal reference pins are provided. Horizontal means a **90° roll around the pin's long axis, then lying flat**; retain the saved pose. They are short hand-test coupons, not replacements for A's long pin.

Remove external brims only; no internal supports are generated. Identify U1/U2/H1/H2 with a marker. Inspect the open thread and the upper-bore roof. Use the same existing reference screw (or the new bare screw off the motor) for all four: start gently, establish the same engagement, and move 3 mm in each direction five times. Stop if a bridge sags or thread catches; do not ream it into a pass.

Then lay each in the U-shaped sliding coupon, same face downward, and slide along it with the same light fingertip pressure. Turn through all four faces and repeat. The open coupon isolates sliding; it is not claimed to constrain every motion. Record thread fit, straightness, seam snagging, face-dependent friction, cracking, and agreement between duplicates. Only a combined thread/sliding improvement supports a horizontal-print preference. A nice exterior alone does not.
