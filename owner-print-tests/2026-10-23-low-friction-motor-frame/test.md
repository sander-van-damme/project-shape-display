## One experiment, with stop-when-explained branches

Use the **same motor, pin, screw, washer, spacer and matched frame halves** throughout. Photograph or label them before disassembly. Disconnect power before touching the mechanism. Do not change current, add lubricant, sand surfaces, cut off the foot or change several interfaces during the comparison.

1. **Identify the actual travel envelope and modified surfaces.** Record pin length, the confirmed long screw's approximate threaded length (baseline 48 mm), absent/present foot, and guide length. Note exactly which rotor-base surfaces were shaved: outside cylinder, underside/washer face, or coupling ribs. Separate observations made before and after shaving; do not shave anything else. Check that the two halves seat together without forcing pegs or moving the motor onto its PCB. A forced/mismatched closure invalidates the comparison. The CAD coordinates below apply only to the current matching geometry; an older or modified part needs its own visible clearance check.

2. **Mark a low position and a position 3 mm higher on the physical pin.** Measure actual rise against the stationary frame with a ruler/paper marks; do not use GUI position or commanded steps. For the current CAD, low pin underside is Z27.9 from the frame bottom, with 0.70 mm above the top of the upper-stop plate. Its foot top has 4.56 mm to the underside of the square guide. At 3 mm actual rise that gap is still 1.56 mm. Never find either end by driving into a stop. If these visible relationships do not fit the printed assembly, record the discrepancy and use an unobstructed smaller interval instead.

3. **Compare manual motion, closed versus pressure relieved, at both marks.** Keep the washer seated. Turn the exposed round rotor gently with fingers; do not twist the square pin or force a jam. Use the same modest hand contact each time. Motor detent is expected when coupled and unpowered; look for an added height-dependent catch. First use normal closure with all split faces seated, motor secure and the same bands/spacer. Then relieve band pressure only enough to compare, keeping parts supported, and reclose. A loose-frame improvement is diagnostic, never a passing operating condition. Record the foot gap/contact, rotor rubbing, motor movement, and smooth/drag/catch at each position.

4. **Compare at the reported high position only while free and supported.** With the already modified long-screw assembly, examine clearances and thread overlap with the frame open first. Then compare gentle manual motion at measured3,6,9,12 and up to15 mm, stopping at the first drag or visible loss of support. These are checkpoints, not a request to force it to15. This diagnoses the existing assembly, **not a release of a15-mm design**. If the wide foot unexpectedly remains, or another obstruction exists, stay within the clear interval and record it; do not remove more material. If geometry/support cannot be established, retain the original15-mm report without recreating it. No powered15-mm test is specified.

5. **Only if it binds inside a visibly unobstructed, engaged range, isolate the first failing interface.** Remove the screw/rotor, keeping the unpowered motor and the same retention shim seated where possible, retain the same frame closure, and slide the pin alone between the same physical marks, closed versus relieved. If it still binds, the guide/closure or pin exterior is implicated; stop there. If the motor must be removed, note that changed preload: a free pin then does not exclude binding under normal motor retention. If it slides freely, test that same screw/pin pair outside the frame at the corresponding engagement positions. A position-dependent catch now implicates thread/bore/scars/straightness. If both pass, assemble rotor + washer without motor or pin and turn it with light downward, then light upward hand bias: closure-sensitive drag implicates the radial land, washer/deck or upper stop. If that passes, restore the motor **without the pin**; added drag implicates coupling/axis alignment or motor retention. Stop at the first clear result. If every separate interface passes but the full assembly binds, record an **alignment-dependent interaction**; no isolated surface has yet been proven guilty.

6. **Powered check only after smooth manual checks and secure normal closure.** Reassemble the same parts and run three slow up/down cycles within the physically marked 0–3 mm interval, using the existing control setup and a documented conservative configuration. Record requested IRUN and readback if available, Vref, microstep mode, speed, and communication status before/after. Do not increase current for this comparison. GUI values alone do not confirm driver state; uncertain current or UART faults make a powered comparison inconclusive. Do not use the observed 18 commanded steps as a revolution calibration. Stop immediately at a stall, shaft axial motion, motor/PCB pressure, slipping gear engagement, thread damage or excessive heat; do not retry with more current.

## Interpretation and stop condition

| Result | Decision |
|---|---|
| Pin alone binds closed and frees when pressure is relieved | Guide/closure is isolated. The bounded two-land relief described in the diagnosis becomes a justified later modification, after matching the actual pin/travel. It is not produced by this task. |
| Pair binds outside frame | Thread, straight bore, print scar or straightness issue; opening guide clearance alone is not a demonstrated remedy. |
| Rotor + washer binds without pin/motor | Inspect witnessed radial/axial contact; retain axial support. A new guide would not isolate this cause. |
| Adding motor causes drag | Preserve the successful C profile; investigate motor/rotor axis alignment and spacer bias rather than making the coupling rigidly tighter. |
| Only complete assembly binds | Coupled misalignment remains; report where it first occurs and do not claim a unique cause. |
| An unexpected retained foot or another visible hard obstruction is found | Identify the actual geometry discrepancy; keep movement within the clear interval. Do not reinterpret a hard stop as insufficient torque or shave it away during this comparison. |
| Freedom improves but motor moves, pin tips, rotor rocks or coupling slips | Reject the condition: reduced friction with lost support is not success. |
| UART error or unconfirmed/reset current during powered comparison | Powered result inconclusive; retain valid manual findings separately. |

**Success for this selected small test** means smooth manual motion without a meaningful high-position increase, properly closed frame, secure motor, adequate rotor and pin support, no shaft motion or loss of thread/gear engagement, and three repeatable powered up/down cycles under documented confirmed configuration without a current increase. That supports the next independently driven pin experiment **only for the tested interval and load**. It does not establish 15 or 40 mm travel, durability, or all 200 motors. Leave the result unknown until performed. Stop after this diagnostic and the owner's decision; no second prototype or electronics work is scheduled.
