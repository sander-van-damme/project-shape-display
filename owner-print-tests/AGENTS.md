# Owner print-test handoff

This folder provides **optional, print-ready Bambu Studio `.3mf` projects for the Bambu Lab X1 Carbon with saved printer, filament and process settings and simple result forms** for Sander. There is no autonomous print queue. An assigned Codex engineer picks a test only within a clearly requested task; Sander may not print soon or ever, without blocking other work.

- Prefer **one small plate** answering one decision. At most three total **READY** jobs, prioritized P1/P2/P3; unused slots need not be filled.
- Follow `TEST-TEMPLATE.md` with `YYYY-MM-DD-<short-description>/test.md` and a **verified Bambu Studio `.3mf` project** in that same directory (not STL-only and not a geometry-only 3MF). Do not send Sander only OpenSCAD/Python or ask him to export CAD. State exact parts, label variants, slicer orientation/support/brim, material, estimated filament/time and a short, safe step-by-step test.

## Mandatory design relevance and assembly handoff (before READY)

- **Decision before artifact.** State the one specific uncertainty the print will resolve, what observation changes the next design decision, and a stop condition. First check whether an existing printed part, a manual fixture or a much smaller coupon answers it. Do not manufacture an elaborate actuator merely to test a coupling or force that can be measured by hand.
- **Physical-scale gate.** List the overall fixture envelope, parts count, printed mass/time, required non-printed parts, support-removal effort, and the **intended full-scale per-channel envelope/pitch**. State clearly whether the coupon fits that geometry. A deliberately oversized force fixture is acceptable only when its isolated measurement is decision-relevant and no smaller/simple fixture reasonably suffices; it does **not** validate packing or array manufacturability. Explain any deviation up front.
- **Do not prematurely choose an actuator.** When the selection/actuation method is still open, briefly compare credible alternatives (including manual/shared mechanical actuation where relevant) for size, complexity, force, power and serviceability before committing to a printed architecture. Do not treat the actuation mechanism chosen for a coupon as the final design, or imply owner approval of one option based on its appearance in a print pack.
- **Owner-readable assembly guide required.** Any READY plate with parts to assemble must contain a simple numbered, hands-on guide in `assembly.md` or `test.md`, starting with a visual parts inventory and the minimal-tools/non-printed-parts checklist. Name parts **exactly as in the supplied 3MF**, including counts and how to distinguish lookalikes. Give orientation, insertion direction, fastening order, what moves/does not, easy first hand-test and warnings against forcing a fit. Put coordinates, datum transforms and detailed calculations in a separate engineering appendix, not in the owner-facing steps.
- **Assembly images are mandatory.** Include at least one clear **labeled exploded assembly image** showing the **actual delivered part geometry**, what connects to what and directional arrows, plus an assembled view; add a cutaway/close-up when a hidden engagement is important. Embed/link these in the steps, not merely a standalone principle schematic or top view. Prefer a lightweight SVG in `images/`; verify the illustration agrees with the print-ready parts. No clear assembly illustration means the pack is **DRAFT, not READY**.
- **Cleanup is part of assembly feasibility.** Explicitly show and explain every support or sacrificial brace that must be removed, which tool and access direction to use, and how to avoid damaging functional surfaces. Favor support-free/easily accessible orientations; if support removal is difficult, time-consuming or unverified, simplify/reorient the design or mark the limitation and keep it DRAFT. Slicer success is not evidence that supports can be removed.
- **A human can actually assemble it.** Before READY, review the full numbered procedure against the delivered mesh/BOM without relying on developer knowledge. Clearly separate printed parts, required purchases/scavenged stock and optional electronics; state whether a partial dry-fit test is possible without them. Avoid unnecessary steps, precision adjustment and exotic hardware when the intended decision does not require them. Physical fit remains unknown until the owner tests it.

- Where relevant include **2–3 tolerance variants and several identical nominal samples** on a small plate. For long slender rods, check support stability through all layers, support removal and functional thread scarring; don't assert an unsupported upright part is qualified because a slicer printed no support path.
- Record printer conditions and actual fit, travel, friction, binding, damage, cleanup and other observations. An unperformed test is **unknown**, never a measured pass or failure; five coupons do not qualify thousands of parts.
- Track status DRAFT / READY / DEFERRED / COMPLETED / RETIRED. On new evidence, update genuine priority and retire stale print offers (preserve historic STLs as evidence) with a reason. No deadline, reminders, purchase or ever-growing backlog.


## Mandatory directory names and Bambu Studio projects

- Every test directory is prefixed **`YYYY-MM-DD-`** (date first introduced, not date of eventual printing or last modification), followed by a short kebab-case description, e.g. `2026-10-15-compact-screw-fit`. Names remain stable when results are added. Update all code includes, scripts and documentation references when relocating a directory.
- Keep owner instructions, assembly/result forms and the primary print-ready `.3mf` at the test root. Put component STLs in `components/` (retired evidence in `components/archive/`), CAD sources in `cad/`, build/review scripts in `scripts/`, JSON settings/reports/provenance in `metadata/`, and illustrations in `images/`. Keep a self-contained software package in `software/` when present. Explain the layout in `test.md`; avoid extra index documents.
- Maintain one file per illustration, preferring SVG for vector drawings; do not keep both `assembly.svg` and `assembly.png`, or byte-identical initial/reopened review images. Keep distinct original/reopened JSON evidence when it verifies different archives. Update generators so they preserve this layout and do not recreate duplicate images.
- When reorganizing an existing pack, update inbound documentation links, CAD includes/imports, script paths and metadata references. Verify reproduction paths and preserve the actual meshes, print project and historical evidence; a directory cleanup alone does not require reslicing an unchanged `.3mf`. Apply this layout to new packs and packs explicitly being reorganized, without an unrequested repository-wide migration.
- For every **new or reissued READY test**, the primary delivery is a **genuine Bambu Studio project `.3mf` for the Bambu Lab X1 Carbon** (normally the 0.4 mm nozzle). It must embed the printable parts, layout/orientation, selected printer/nozzle, filament, bed and **actual process settings**. Merely wrapping STL meshes in a 3MF file, or supplying an STL plus written recommendations, does not qualify. The STL/CAD sources may still be retained for engineering reproduction.
- Explicitly configure and document relevant **layer and first-layer heights, Arachne/Classic wall generator, line width and wall counts, infill, speeds, accelerations, supports and their placement, brim/raft, XY/hole/elephant-foot compensation, cooling and print order**. Thin threaded walls require particular care; never assume Bambu Studio defaults preserve them.
- **Reopen the saved 3MF in Bambu Studio, select/verify the X1 Carbon and slice it.** Inspect actual wall paths, thread continuity, open bores, bed adhesion and whether supports fill the threaded cavities. Log the Bambu Studio version, exact saved profile values, and slicer-estimated time/material (not physical observations). Don't claim READY if a usable configured 3MF and meaningful X1C slicer check could not be provided; clearly record the blocker instead.
- Owner results must distinguish saved recommended settings from **settings actually printed**. When settings are modified, record the changes or attach the printed 3MF. Older completed print packs with only STL remain valid historical evidence; do not fabricate a missing 3MF retroactively.
