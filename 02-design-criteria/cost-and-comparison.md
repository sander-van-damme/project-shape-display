Purchased/nonprinted components: <$200 ideal; $200–400 acceptable; $400–500 last resort; >$500 unacceptable. Count motors, electronics, sensors, bearings, fasteners, wiring, guides, power supplies and all other bought hardware.

Printed parts have no formal cost ceiling. Still report material, print complexity, time, failures and assembly burden when comparing practical total cost.

Compare: D&D usefulness, pitch, area, travel, surface gaps, bought cost, actuator/wiring/power scaling, full/local update time, reliability, durability, buildability and small-prototype testability. Distinguish assumptions, sourced facts, calculations, simulation, CAD and physical measurements. Reliability and coupon testability are gates.
