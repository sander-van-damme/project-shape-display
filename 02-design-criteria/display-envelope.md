**Owner D&D geometry (2026-10-14):** The game square is **25.4 mm**, miniatures must stand on the square floors, and **thin walls belong on the LINES between squares**. A corridor one square wide must fit its intended physical mini base between the two real, nonzero-thickness inner wall faces. Prove dimensional wall/floor/mini clearance, cap support and neighbor compatibility before promoting a pitch. The 12.7-mm half-square alternative is only a control, not the default; finer grids are not automatically a pass either. See [current priorities](current-prototype-priorities.md).

**Reference surface, not a mandatory resolution:** approximately 400 × 400 mm at 5.08-mm pitch (one fifth of a 25.4-mm / 1-inch D&D square), around 80 × 80 / 6,400 cells. **Explore coarser grids**, e.g. 6.35 mm = one quarter of a D&D square, around 63 × 63 / 4,000 cells over 400 mm, or other playable resolutions. Coarser cells may reduce printed parts, complexity and update duration but may thicken walls, merge corridors or lose useful cover detail. Measure the actual gameplay consequences with miniatures and alternative heightmaps rather than requiring maximum fineness. Surface size, cell count and shape are design variables.

**Reference travel:** ~40 mm useful terrain height difference; not a fixed minimum and not necessarily measured upward from a fully lowered home position. Compare a **raised neutral/mid-travel home position with genuine upward and downward motion** against minimum-height home and rise-only movement. A neutral plane inside 40 mm total travel cannot offer a 40-mm excursion in *both* directions without more range. Consider longer pins, underside clearance, guide length, load retention, safe end stops, home/re-zero procedure and the initial setup burden. Adjust travel to visible hills/walls/cliffs and playable depressions rather than preserving a nominal stroke regardless of value.

For **whichever pitch is chosen**, the terrain-cell size constrains column tops, not necessarily shared selectors/memory/locks. Actuators may live under rows, banks, tiles or outside the board. Count repeated features at the **actual proposed cell count**, as well as at the 6,400-cell reference.

**D&D representation is gameplay-first, not artwork-height fidelity.** The movable columns should create **meaningful solid terrain**: traversable earth and hills, substantial rock formations, cliffs/deep chasms, stairs, raised platforms, and especially thin, readable walls and barriers. These forms convey movement, cover, line of sight, elevation and miniature support. Do **not** automatically lift every illustrated object: trees, bushes, tiny loose stones, decorative rocks, furniture, clutter and textures often look like false solid walls when approximated by pin clusters. Use ordinary removable props for objects whose physical game role matters, or visual artwork/optional overhead projection for nonstructural detail. Even a tree that blocks sight may be better represented by a clear game token than by a misleading pin-built wall. Projection is **not yet qualified**; assess registration on uneven terrain, miniatures/occlusion, shadows, readability, setup effort and cost before relying on it.

**Depth depiction needs deliberate playable alternatives.** Raising most surrounding ground to make a river or chasm appear lower on upward-only pins is one legitimate encoding, not an automatic product requirement. Consider, for each map, a visual flat waterway, selective physical banks, bridges and meaningful obstacles, or genuinely elevated plateau/cliff geometry when the depth is important for play. Compare what the players actually see, where miniatures can stand and move, and which boundaries matter to the Game Master before concluding how many cells *must* rise. The best physical/visual split is an untested product question and should be informed by owner/player evaluation, not assumed from a published 2D picture.

**Tabletop ergonomics and transport (secondary preferences, not present pass/fail gates):** aim for a self-contained display that can be carried and placed neatly on a normal table. Minimize total stack height, mass, protrusions and setup burden, particularly in the actuator layer; horizontal/remote motor packaging can reduce vertical depth, but count the resulting cables, gears, tolerances and service cost. User preference: if two comparably viable architectures exist, prefer the thinner/more portable one. A functional, credible prototype comes first; do not discard it merely because it initially sits too tall. A flush-with-table installation is not expected from a portable prototype.

**Owner preference, 2026-10-13 (LAB-384/LAB-386):** retain physical river banks and a physical ravine in the next research workload. Sander wants software-configurable flexibility between faster updates and larger drops; both illustrated depths could be enjoyable, and a larger drop might be more immersive. This selects a capability to investigate, not exact pitch, depth, neutral, stroke, actuator, timing or purchase approval. E-258 compares 20/30-mm depth presets at fixed ground, charges initial preparation and switching, and preserves the simpler 40-mm-travel control. Software settings must stay within mechanically qualified reach/support limits; changing coordinates cannot add travel.
