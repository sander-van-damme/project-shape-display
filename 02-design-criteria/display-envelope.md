Surface: approximately 400 × 400 mm; square/rectangular columns at approximately 5.08 mm or finer pitch (one fifth of a 25.4 mm D&D square). Scale: approximately 79–80 columns/axis, 6,200–6,400 total.

Travel: provisional minimum 40 mm usable.

Surface pitch constrains column tops, not internal selectors/memory/locks. Internal machinery may span cells or live at row, bank, tile or external-writer scale.

D&D representation: prioritize playable 3D walls, terrain relief, large obstacles and miniature support. Smaller props (e.g., tiny furnishings, chests), markings and textures may be represented as projected/overlaid imagery instead of distinct mechanical elevations; some larger props may still be physically represented. An overhead projector is an **optional complement**, not a validated design requirement. Assess alignment on changing heights, shadows/occlusion, image readability and cost before counting it as a product feature.

**Tabletop ergonomics and transport (secondary preferences, not present pass/fail gates):** aim for a self-contained display that can be carried and placed neatly on a normal table. Minimize total stack height, mass, protrusions and setup burden, particularly in the actuator layer; horizontal/remote motor packaging can reduce vertical depth, but count the resulting cables, gears, tolerances and service cost. User preference: if two comparably viable architectures exist, prefer the thinner/more portable one. A functional, credible prototype comes first; do not discard it merely because it initially sits too tall. A flush-with-table installation is not expected from a portable prototype.
