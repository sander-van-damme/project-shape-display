Gate: what must work **at every cell of the proposed playable display**? Approximately 6,400 is the original reference, **not a mandatory count**. A coarser quarter-square or other resolution can reduce replicated failure sites and cost substantially, but must preserve useful walls, miniature placement and legibility. Minimize critical precision/compliant contacts per cell and the **total actual replicated burden**; prefer simple passive columns plus accessible shared mechanisms.

Treat as high-risk, requiring explicit evidence rather than a blanket ban on new mechanisms: single-extrusion moving features, force-critical tiny springs, repeated submillimetre precision, friction hold where hard stops are possible, tight global tolerances and undetectable unrecoverable cell faults. Prefer positive engagement, compression load paths, generous clearances, accessible replaceable parts and local recovery.

Audit each architecture: repeated moving parts, precision contacts/cell, compliant elements, wear interfaces/service life, tolerance-sensitive interactions, correlated and single-cell failures, and service access. Label the evidence for each.

After computational screening and any justified calibration, physical qualification ladder: single cell → small full-pitch array (e.g. 5×5) → complete bank/module → multiple banks → full machine. Validate fit, neighbor interaction, load, repeated cycling, reset, correlated failures and timing before scaling. A calculated or CAD pass is not physical qualification.

Sander's informal rod/grid printing experience reports visible piece-to-piece fit variation. Treat tolerance sensitivity **inside every replicated pin** as a first-order risk; a robust repeated thread or passive feature is not banned, but must work across credible print errors and cycling. Shared higher-precision tooling, camera-based correction and controlled homing are legitimate alternatives to per-pin adjustment only when they do not replace necessary physical support, selector isolation, wear life or safe recovery with assumptions.
