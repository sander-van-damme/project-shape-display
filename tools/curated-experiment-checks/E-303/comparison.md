# A–H comparison and decision

**Evidence checked 23 October 2026.** None of these clutches has new physical Shape Display evidence. “Pursue” below means the one prepared passive friction test, not an architecture change. Counts include the baseline pin/screw and stationary support functions where stated; manufactured pieces and independent motion are separate measures. Electronics and bonds are additional. Unknown values remain unknown.

## Mechanical complexity first

| Family / principle | Count and independent motion | Materials / manufacturing | Main advantage and failure modes | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A — always-contact electromagnetic friction** | Concrete concept: 13 pieces plus a selected PET shim = **14**, including pin/screw and two housing pieces; two adhesive bonds are operations. Two independent rotating assemblies, translating pin, one elastic armature function. No separate sliding sleeve. | Printed support/input/carrier/bobbins, four ordinary steel pieces, two wound coils. Fine flexure, bonding and shimming remain demanding. | Stationary wiring, inexpensive metal, natural slip. OFF preload, remanence, flexure creep, parasitic thrust drag, two working gaps and coil heat are serious. | **Pursue passive interface test only**; best fit to the user's priorities among A/B, but no qualified compact actuator. |
| **B1 — powered two-electrode electrostatic friction** | Two rotors plus compliant film/plate and electrical contacts. Complete count depends on shared-conductor arrangement; do not assume fewer parts than B2 merely because its electrode is unsplit. | Printed holders; metal/film electrodes; thin dielectric; two reliable electrical paths to independently rotating sides. High-performance research uses unusual coating/patterning. | Very low ideal holding current. Floating/brush contacts, film conformity, charge trapping, dielectric failure and release dominate. | **Defer energized build**; compare plain-film friction passively if materials are available. |
| **B2 — split powered input / floating output** | 12 clutch pieces + pin + two housing halves = **15 before closure/shims**; illustrated single-cell closure adds four fasteners = **19**. Two rotors, flexing film and two compliant brush contacts. | Two input sectors, PET dielectric, metallized floating film, two tracks, two brushes, two rotor links; no custom-machined metal. Patterning/bonding/insulation still repeated processing. | Removes output wire; keeps two input rotary connections. One-quarter B1 force at equal terminal voltage; uneven leakage/stray capacitance changes floating potential. | **Defer energized build**; promising electrical principle, weakest low-cost contact/pitch assumptions. |
| **C — bistable magnetic sleeve** | **≥12** pieces including baseline, before leads/shims/closure; input + output + sliding sleeve are three moving bodies with two independent rotations; the keyed sleeve also translates. One PM, coil, two pole/stop regions and return path counted. | Printed keyed sleeve/housing, standard ring magnet/steel/copper. A complete switching circuit and force-position margin are not established. | No continuous coil hold. Switching margin, magnetic coupling to neighbors, latch force, phase alignment and positive release remain open. Dogs do not slip; friction version adds preload/drag concerns. | **Defer as main mechanical fallback** if A's holding heat is unacceptable. |
| **D — conventional electromagnetic** | Rough functional count **10–14**, integration-dependent, including baseline/supports; two rotors and an axial armature/flexible return, or a moving dog sleeve. | Coil, pole/yoke, armature, return/flexible torque link, independent bearings/supports. A printed diaphragm integrates a part but retains its function. | Defined separation can reduce OFF drag. Wear gap, return force, alignment, coil heat and engagement stroke remain; dogs need phase alignment and obstruction protection. | **Defer reference/fallback**; no reason to elaborate another print. |
| **E — magnetic hysteresis** | Complete count unknown; at least two supported rotors, specialty ring, rotating pole structure, coil and return circuit beyond baseline. No sliding selector. | Semi-hard magnetic rotor and shaped poles; no verified standard cheap set found. | Contactless smooth torque is attractive; residual magnetism, pole manufacture and slip heat are not removed. Ordinary soft-steel washers do not substitute for a characterized hysteresis ring. | **Reject under standard-metal constraint.** |
| **F — electropermanent / AlNiCo** | Magnetic subassembly alone: two magnets + two poles + coil. Add two rotating assemblies, friction/compliance, supports and pulse electronics; complete standard-part count unknown. | Real miniature demonstration used custom-cut/ground magnets and waterjet/EDM poles. Stock cheaper AlNiCo has incompatible dimensions. | Persistent state avoids hold power. Pulse current, residual OFF field, special metal processing and complete clutch parts remain. | **Defer, no prototype**; new low catalogue AlNiCo price does not solve manufacture. |
| **G — MR fluid** | Two rotors, coil/return, chamber/cap, fluid, independent supports and normally two shaft seals, fill/retention; complete count unknown. No translating selector. | MR fluid and compatible containment/seals; printed shell alone is insufficient. | Controllable shear torque; low-temperature viscosity, settling, leakage, seals and 6,400 fills/repairs dominate. | **Reject for repeated containment complexity.** |
| **H — magnetic powder** | Two rotors, coil/return, powder chamber/charge, powder barriers, closure and independent supports; complete count unknown. No translating selector. | Magnetic powder, precise gap and particle containment. | Controllable slip without armature stroke; residual drag, powder distribution/migration, heat and servicing remain. | **Reject for drag/containment burden.** |
