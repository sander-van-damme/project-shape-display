# E-303 — Decision for the owner

**23 October 2026. Keep DES-009 as the primary direction.** None of the alternatives is yet a demonstrated cheap clutch at5.08-mm pitch. The next useful work is one small measurement, not a full array.

1. **Which mechanism looks most promising?** A, electromagnetic friction, is the best candidate for the first inexpensive interface test: ordinary steel and plastic, stationary coil wiring and useful slip behavior. Its actuator is less promising than the initial “washer plus coil” picture: independent screw support requires a compliant force path, and useful magnetic force costs power. B offers low holding current but needs thin, conforming dielectric films and powered rotating contacts. C remains the fallback if a real sliding selector and pulse driver are worth the added parts.

2. **Can it realistically fit5.08 mm?** Not demonstrated. A4.7-mm bare disc leaves0.38 mm; a5-mm disc leaves only0.08 mm. The complete standard-part magnetic concept is12.4 mm wide. Two/four height levels give7.184/10.16-mm same-level spacing, but larger discs also collide with neighboring through-shafts. The drawings expose these obstacles rather than claiming a finished dense design.

3. **What does a clutch contain and cost?** The A concept contains **14 discrete pieces including pin/screw, supports and selected shim**, plus two counted bonding operations and electronics. A normalized repeated-parts/electronics example is **about €0.80 per cell**, roughly **€5,100 for6,400**; a broad planning band is€0.60–1.50 per cell. B2 has15 pieces before closure/shims,19 in the illustrated single-cell housing, and an uncertain **€0.59 example** (broadly€0.40–1.20). Its two input brushes remain. These include baseline printed pin/screw and support allocations; shared supplies/transmission, labor, yield, taxes and delivery are excluded. They are planning estimates, not quotations or pitch-qualified products. [Full BOMs and supplier prices](components-and-costs.md) expose each allowance.

4. **What torque can we expect?** We cannot yet predict measured torque. The larger A reference calculates roughly **0.10 mN·m at0.55 W** before leakage or flexure closure force, with severe initial coil heating. The compact B2 plain-PET model at300 V gives **0.043 mN·m at ideal zero gap**, falling to **0.0077 at a5-µm gap**. Illustrative light/moderate screw-start scenarios require about **0.060/0.343 mN·m**. The existing motorized pin proves movement, not either torque target. The specialized20-mm electrostatic research cannot be scaled directly into a cheap5-mm PET clutch.

5. **What remains uncertain?** Actual screw start/run torque and backdrive under load; minimum nonselected breakaway; interface OFF drag, wear and slip variation; real magnetic force/gaps and coil temperature; film conformity, residual adhesion and electrical-contact life; assembly yield and complete pitch-compatible support. A slipping clutch does not automatically preserve height or guarantee safe finger force.

6. **What would justify shifting attention away from DES-009?** Measured bidirectional movement of a supported screw, low enough OFF drag to leave an unloaded neighbor still, repeatable slip and release after wear, retained height, a complete fit around neighboring shafts, and a credible assembled cost/thermal budget better than the traveling bank. No such combined evidence exists yet.

7. **What is the smallest meaningful physical test?** Use the [single passive friction fixture](../../../owner-print-tests/2026-10-23-clutch-friction-screen/test.md): compare the small/large contact faces under weighed loads, measure torque in both directions, restrain the output while continuing input rotation, then repeat after20 slow slip revolutions. Start with PLA and steel; add PET only if available. No coil or high voltage is needed. Record instrument resolution; an unresolved OFF reading is not zero. Compare any useful result with a measured torque from the existing screw before approving actuator work.
