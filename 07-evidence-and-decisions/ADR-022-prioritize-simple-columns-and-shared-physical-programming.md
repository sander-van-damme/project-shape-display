# Prioritize simple columns and shared physical programming

Primary research now favors simple repeated columns and shared, reusable,
accessible mechanical programming: masks, encoded layers and common support.
E-208–E-215 local toe/return/capture/guidance refinement is dormant. Their
historical “next” suggestions are not an active agenda. Reopening requires a
concrete change that removes the repeated manufacturing burden and a competitive
whole-machine ledger; another nominal clearance result is insufficient.
Canonical evidence and executable sources remain intact.

E-216 compares resident shutter stops with reusable programmed row heads. Neither
is a machine survivor: four planes repeat 25,600 shutter/retention sites; the
explicit reusable stepped-mask writer reaches the deadline only with hundreds
of independent setting lanes and unresolved support forces. Preserve the common
support/reusable-state principle, but park these embodiments before detailed
contact work. E-217 constructs binary-stop/common-stroke and commutated-cam writers. Their
<30-s sampled cases still need hundreds of controlled gates; binary encoding
also multiplies retained bits and differential pushers. Park both before local
refinement. Next examine media that bears column loads directly, with a finite
mixed-height supported exchange before any detailed writer work. The purpose
is to remove frictional temporary grip state and duplicated old-height encoding,
not to hide writing time in a spare cassette. Support, unchanged-neighbor
isolation and complete 6,400-cell cost/replication remain decisive gates.

Every deep mechanism investigation must first justify its system-level value
against a simpler comparator, including replication, assembly, correlated process
errors, service, arbitrary-map time and purchased-cost allocations. Unknown
manufacturing capability cannot count as a pass. Narrow repeated margins or
unaffordable channel multiplicity redirect work immediately; they are not merely
report caveats. This changes research priority, not product constraints or the
need for eventual physical qualification.
