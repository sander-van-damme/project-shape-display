# Park the bounded direct-head capture route before machine evaluation

**Decision:** close the studied direct-head mechanical gate with a scoped
rejection. Retire its dependent complete-system timing/cost evaluation. No
complete powered head survives; no fabrication, purchase or requirement change
is justified. This is an allocation decision, not proof that direct heads or
wider internal machinery cannot work.

**Basis:** E-123's centred pad cannot force lowering in its declared drag
scenario; its C shoe's nominal acquisition does not survive the finite guided
pose in E-124. E-124's bilateral cup changes the force path but leaves retention
and power absent. E-125 supplies closed channels and capped pins: adjacent
retained heads collide at the tested compact pitch. Legal 10.16-mm spacing
escapes that collision, yet taking up the actual closing walls causes the rigid
jaw to miss the upper shoulder and enter the foot side under the declared
.025-mm inward face error. These are different failures of three implemented
capture routes, not three failed complete machines.

The E-125 loaded-path rejection does not depend on the reduced contact model's
invalid fixed nodes. Direct reconstruction from the generated right-pad bounds
at q=.793470869 gives x_min=.971 mm and z_min=1.194223305 mm against a foot
ending at x=.975, z=1.2 mm. Their 1.6-mm shared depth gives
(.975−.971)×(1.2−1.194223305)×1.6 = .000036971 mm³ intersection,
matching the polygon calculation. This is an analytical self-check of rigid
geometry, not independent external validation or a physical jam measurement.

**Consequences:** retain the executable acquisition, retention, contact and
support-loss witnesses in E-123–125; stop tolerance-only refinement of this cup.
Do not inherit earlier conditional bank timing or motor budgets. The indexed
ground bolt cannot arrest every intermediate height, and positive jaw capture
does not arrest a backdrivable axis. Power, powered lock set/reset, retained
selection, finite readback, proof and safe recovery remain unimplemented; their
absence is not evidence that every possible implementation fails. They need no
further implementation on the rejected capture route. Preserve the simple
direct-head comparator without granting machine feasibility.

**Reopening:** require changed contact/channel topology or a complete sparse
head arrangement that handles loaded acquisition across the declared bounds,
arbitrary neighbour heights and full travel. A viable continuation must then
supply positive carrier arrest while ground support is withdrawn, powered ground
transfer, retained selection through proof/withdrawal, finite readback and
isolated fault recovery. More head spacing alone or a narrower unsupported
error box does not meet this condition. Separate closure/take-up, relocated
joints and moving-edge compliant capture remain untested alternatives, not
admitted survivors. Portfolio selection should compare their complete repeated
and shared machinery burden against changed force-transmission principles before
funding another detailed section sweep.

**Residual limits:** deterministic bounds are not measured X1C priors or yield
estimates. No full-board performance, cost, material strength, wear/lifetime,
readback reliability or hardware qualification has been established. Retiring
machine evaluation records a failed prerequisite; it does not establish failure
of the product requirements or end the research program.
