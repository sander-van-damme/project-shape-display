# Conclude chamber-side hydraulic addressing with scoped exclusions

**Decision:** park the tested mechanical-coincidence, single wet-bank and dual
dry-poppet implementations. Close this research campaign without selecting a
machine. Do not continue dry-head detail, leakage qualification or another
passage sweep. No print, purchase or product-constraint change.

**Basis:** E-100–103 exposed moving-support/contact/reset contradictions;
E-104–107 exposed mixed directions and load-dependent fine-flow scheduling.
E-108's changed dual-bank, ≤40-open-channel schedule escapes a new pump-starvation
witness and retains a conditional 23.387-s bound. E-109 implements a distinct
finite two-poppet circuit subset: its catalog spring cannot maintain closure
across the inherited pressure box, while the priced 160-servo route exceeds
$500 before any other purchases. Two stems also double the prior minimum gland
count. Finite-volume integration verifies the stem-sweep bound; nominal servo
closure does not meet the inherited response allocation. These are scoped mechanism and
procurement failures, not proof hydraulic height memory is impossible.

| Portfolio comparison | Decision-relevant benefit | Remaining exclusion or conditional gate |
|---|---|---|
| Hydraulic dry banks, A-014 | Shared fluid performs 40-mm work; chunking gives a conditional time window with 160 heads | Resident seats/seals/returns, closure-volume and affordable complete heads unresolved; stop this assembly |
| Wet docking, A-014 | Reuses metering and reduces resident stem interfaces | Tested single bank retains >31-s fixed-metering cases; acquisition pressure/air/volume and selective isolation unresolved |
| Direct rack heads, A-013 | Positive support, 18.854-s prior four-bank allocation | 320 complete heads; sourced servo route also over budget; no automatic winner |
| Common-pressure bellows, A-020 | Removes sliding fluid seals and per-cell flow valves | E-064 rolling geometry remains process-sensitive; bilateral capture and pressure/frame reactions unresolved |
| Thermal collars, A-018 | Different support-memory principle may replace small pawls/returns | Minimum keyed load-bearing volume, heat removal/crosstalk and affordable addressed heating remain poorly resolved |

No credible complete-system dominance follows from these unequal evidence
levels. Favorable timings are conditional calculations, not physical performance.
Printed parts have no formal price cap, but 6,400 repeated interfaces still carry
manufacturing, assembly, leak-test, service and lifetime burdens.

**Reopening:** changed complete selection/return/isolation with a credible joint
bought budget, then finite transitions and revised pressure-volume/response/
full-local timing. For wet docking, include conditioning, selective check opening,
source isolation, chamber closure, dump and withdrawal. A pressure-balanced
valve, integrated elastic return or custom cheap drive is a new investigation;
none is credited as an already available fix. Preserve reproducible failures
and E-108's chunking bound. Seal/process/hardware qualification remains separate.

**Next exploration allocation:** bound A-018's keyed microvolume thermal collar
as an enabling support element, comparing fusible inserts, thermoplastic collars
and ordinary mechanical latches. Begin with inverse geometry/load/energy limits;
only survivors warrant transient heat/contact/crosstalk and complete addressing/
capture schedules. Thermal collars do not repair ADR-017's serial-height-group
failure by themselves. Reject or redirect if robust load-bearing volume and heat
removal cannot coexist at pitch and cycle time. This materially different
support principle is preferable to another nominal poppet or servo-price sweep.
Campaign administration and execution bounds belong in Paperclip.
