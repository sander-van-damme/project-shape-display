# Close the studied thermal-support search without selecting a machine

**Decision:** park the generated open capillary bridge and technically conclude
the keyed thermal-support campaign. Retire its dependent transient-thermal and
full-machine investigations, which had no accepted containment survivor. No
fabrication, purchase or calibration request; no product requirement changes.
Paperclip's administrative recovery guard remains its owner's responsibility.

**Basis:** E-110's wet translating keys export material and obstruct when frozen;
E-111's long enclosed bath adds substantial inventory; E-112/113's short-stroke
capsules add finite packaging, film/seal and process burdens. E-114 changes
containment principle to open nonwetting capillary reservoirs. Its nominal
pressure/volume pass is real within the model, and slower acceleration can
rescue one uncertain pressure case. However its generated short-stroke stem
still carries possible liquid from the fixed lip into a cold guide; retention,
return and refreeze cannot be claimed from static contact angles. No credible
repeated manufacturing/process advantage over a dry positive stop has been
established. This is a scoped engineering allocation decision, not proof that
all thermal locks fail or that every E-114 uncertainty bound is physically real.

**Reopening:** a materially changed complete heated catch/return/isolation path
or demonstrated low-carryout interface, paired with affordable manufacture and
bounded dynamic wetting/oxide response, can reopen the capillary topology.
Show per-pore conservation/pinning, complete freeze load contact, long-term
creep and regional recovery before thermal tuning or machine schedules. New
sealed-capsule work also needs changed placement/return and a credible compatible
process. Smaller dimensions, ideal drainage and another nominal seal sweep
are insufficient. Analytical acceptance would still not qualify hardware.

**Next portfolio opportunity:** positive mechanical service support whose
selection and reset use shared energy, with accessible replaceable dry stops.
Compare a grounded crossbolt and gravity-seated rigid pawl against an over-center
load-isolated command latch, starting with actual unload/reseat/half-selection
transitions and complete channel count. Addressing arithmetic alone is not a
mechanism; preserve A-013's expensive actuator and prior serial/grouping failures.
First discriminate whether a single command can persist through support proof
and reset without repeated independent positioning drives or high-force cell
springs. Research administration belongs in Paperclip; no complete architecture
is selected here and a further campaign must avoid duplicating retired selectors.
