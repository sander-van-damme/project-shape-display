## Actuation, observation and scale boundaries

- E-260: independent pair replacement matters. Sealed module disposal amplifies
  correlated scrap. At4096/6400,32h maker budget leaves18.08/10.49s handling/pair
  under stated setup/repair allocations; an extra minute adds68/107h. No measured
  process yield. Track lots, finishing, assembly and repair, not just part price.
- E-261/E-262: guide span/lean, engagement and rotary stability matter throughout
  travel. Assumed soft supports can reduce modeled critical-speed bounds to~522rpm
  or lose stability. 850/2000/3000rpm are not earned rates. Wider polymer or steel
  core does not fix support-limited modes; tiny core ligaments add repeated burden.
- E-264/E-265: rotating nut avoids long rotating screw but closed sockets need
  long extraction; side drive needs long shafts whose bending defeats the sampled
  torque closure. Extra selective radial slides are not free. Park those layouts.
- E-266/E-267: short heads/lower concurrency reduce installed channels, but whole
  scenes and initial neutral remain slow. Local reveal models are conditional
  convenience-map windows, not measured typical play. Stop schedule/bank sweeps
  without changed drive/support/BOM evidence; prep cannot be hidden as free.
- E-268/E-269: fine optical slots sever guide/support webs. Coarse interstitial
  rays nominally clear but conflict with full-span ribs and uncertain keeper
  geometry. Opaque shared support is a useful control; repair/access/capture remain
  unconstructed. Do not notch every rib or reopen micro-aperture optimization.
- E-270: post-undock top sensing escapes solid supports, but covered pins return
  unknown; motor counts cannot prove actual height or engagement. Common camera
  calibration, whole-pitch identity and stale data matter. Passive central-patch
  decoder failed under blur; no further pixel-margin sweep or per-pin marks
  without independent contrast evidence. Shared references help only their modeled
  calibration modes. No qualified100-ms optical read, even at coarse pitch.
- E-271/E-272: routed16-output head is costly/complex; few direct spaced spindles
  remove belts/risers and remain the simple actuation control. Fine routed hardware
  collides. Coarse four/eight-spindle whole waits still depend on physical rate,
  docking, support and fresh actual-height checks. No head purchase/selection.
- E-273/E-274/E-275: shared rack, circulating friction band and fixed-rotor axial
  coupling are parked. Respectively: rigid phase mismatch/large swept envelope;
  band tension causes excessive axial docking rub; friction-face squeeze/keeper
  drag and resident lift have no complete survivor. Fewer motors did not erase
  selectors, alignment, retention and shared transmission interfaces. Do not add
  another pocket/tooth/spring or fit refinement to these embodiments.
