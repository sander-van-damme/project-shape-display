# Simple columns do not make physical programming free

**Park both tested embodiments before local development.** Four resident
shutter planes repeat 25,600 support switches. Reusable row heads remove
individual resident locks but the explicit writer needs 256–1,280 setter lanes
in sampled <30-s cases. Prioritize a different shared encoding/writer.

Input main `375d168`. Reproduce with `python3
tools/curated-experiment-checks/E-216/screen.py` (standard library; no files
written). Evidence: **architecture hypotheses, deterministic logical replay,
kinematic calculations and explicitly assumed bounds; self-review only**.
No physical measurements, process distributions, finite CAD, purchased quotes
or reliability qualification. Five heights at 0/10/20/30/40 mm are a comparison
workload, not an approved restriction of arbitrary terrain resolution.

## Two materially different support architectures

**S — resident shutter-stop stack.** A smooth, flat-ended column descends through
four height planes. A closed sliding shutter at 10/20/30/40 mm supports its end;
all-open selects the fixed zero stop. A canonical code closes the desired plane
only. Each cell therefore needs four independently retained binary shutters,
with guides and set/reset access. A scanning writer physically pushes each
shutter to a hard endpoint and reads its state. Grounded plane ribs, not writer
force, carry service loads. Hard endpoints do not prevent a slider drifting
away: detents or a separate clamping system remain necessary and counted.

A controlled pickup must raise columns above the stack, hold during writing,
then lower onto stops. A flat platen cannot acquire arbitrary old heights;
differential pickups remain unbuilt. Regional writing needs selective pickup
and shutter access without withdrawing supports under unchanged columns. Faults require arrested pickup support; source-loss arrest, actual engagement
proof and loaded-foot clearance are unconstructed. No transfer pass follows.

**R — common row comb with a reusable, mechanically programmed holding head.**
Each identical column has five broad shelf grooves and a long smooth grip land.
A translating resident comb enters all 80 columns of a row at one frame height;
the corresponding groove selects each column's height. Frame-backed comb teeth
carry vertical loads; a positive row keeper prevents sideways withdrawal in
service. Two <=40-column sections fit the 256-mm print envelope, joined to one
row motion. No individual resident pawl, return spring, socket or key is used.

A travelling head first grips **every** column of the row at its actual height,
proves takeover, then withdraws the comb. Its 80 stationary holding grips retain
unchanged cells without commanded height movement. Another 80 independently
retained travelling grips share one elevator. Two reusable mechanical program
arrays carry old and target levels: old has six states including BYPASS for
unchanged cells; target has five. Each slider has 3-mm-spaced states (15-mm range). An unbuilt phased cam
selects transfers; grip states need independent retention beyond the masks.

Acquire changed positive-old columns, descend through their old-height groups,
and deposit each at zero into the stationary grips. Acquire positive-target
columns at zero, rise through target-height groups and deposit them. Return
empty, insert/prove the common comb, then release the head. This replaces 6,400 individual resident releases with 80 common releases;
friction, retention and wear move into B serviceable heads. Masks are physically
overwritten for each visited row, including bypass; no prepared media is credited.

Unbuilt: mask drive/retention, reader cam to independent grip latch, pressure
equalization, grip reach over 40-mm travel/up to 80-mm relative motion, comb
takeover without lifting unchanged columns, keeper, carriage packing/guidance,
cables, sensors and source-loss arrest. These are architecture gates.

R needs **160 grip retention functions/head** and another 160 slider retention
functions: springs/latches, keepers, bearings, bolts and cables remain unpriced.
S has 25,600 unresolved retention functions. Printed mechanisms are not free
assembly or reliability. R trades resident locks for reusable friction/state
machinery and row-wide failures; it is not yet cheaper or more reliable.

Illustrative solid 3×3×80-mm column stock is **4,608 cm³**, before caps/grooves;
four 406.4-mm-square, 2-mm-thick plane blanks add **1,321 cm³** before apertures,
ribs and guides. These are stock-volume scenarios, not CAD/minimum mass. Effective extrusion
5/10/15 mm³/s implies 256/128/85.3 h for columns, excluding travel/cooling/setup;
10% scrap multiplies by 1/.9. Tall stock needs orientation/straightness control;
flat combs/sliders need layer/contact design. Segmented datums add common bias.
At five seconds/shutter S adds 35.6 assembly hours. One five/ten/thirty-second
operation per column adds **8.89/17.78/53.33 h**. At B=8, 30 seconds/grip adds
10.67 h and ten seconds/mask slider adds 3.56 h. These are assumed sensitivities.
Monolithic printing can reduce placements, not inspection/wear/reprint sites.

## Executed timing and support discrimination

Replay checks **625 two-cell old/new maps** and detects absent takeover,
deposit and final-comb supports. An 80×80 workload contains all 25 pairs per
row: unchanged heights persist, changed cells remain logically supported and
finish correctly. Eight 10-mm elevator moves total 80 mm/row. **28,800 support
observations** are required. These are ideal flags, not built sensors/contacts.

For each setting lane, every value incurs worst 15-mm state positioning,
2-mm engagement and 2-mm withdrawal, plus assumed 5-ms readback. q lanes write
the 80 old values and 80 target values in separate passes. Groups also incur
5.08-mm indexing. All moves are rest-to-rest trapezoids at a=500/2,000/8,000
mm/s² and v<=200 mm/s; no zero-time cam or simultaneous writer is granted.
An additional .5 s/visited row covers dock, comb, registration, grip proof and
final readback, plus .020 s/elevator stop and actual 5.08-mm row transport.
These **unverified allowances** must absorb their real mechanisms. Maps include
2 s common processing/homing/settling and one full worst-row retry. No overlap
or offline writing is credited. Contiguous banks execute their own rows; a
local patch does not receive all B heads for free.

## Manufacturing, force, faults and sensitivity

All following inputs are **competing bounds**, not X1C/PLA priors. For one
functional comb insertion, add common print/datum bias, local dimensional
error and head/row skew: (.05+.05+.05), (.15+.10+.10), (.30+.20+.20) mm give
**.15/.35/.70 mm** adverse error. An illustrative 4-mm groove and 2-mm blade
has 1-mm centered half-clearance, leaving .85/.65/.30 mm before deformation
and debris. This is a broad-slot hypothesis, not proof of loaded reseating;
service bearing position and unchanged-cell settling are different functions.
The error tipping point is their 1-mm sum, not a universal print tolerance.
Common bias can jam a whole 80-cell comb; iid sampling would hide that mode.
A 3-mm shutter aperture around a 2-mm foot has only .5-mm side clearance and
fails the .70-mm error scenario. Enlarging it costs support land/web area and
must be re-evaluated. Neither nominal inequality qualifies fabrication.

For provisional cell loads F=1/3.27/10 N (not product load specifications),
whole-board supports carry 6,400/20,928/64,000 N if every cell sees that load;
a row carries 80/261.6/800 N. Avoid mistaking this severe load envelope for
ordinary miniature loading. A frictional temporary head requires summed normal
force at least `80 F/mu`. At F=3.27 N, mu=.1/.2/.4 gives **2,616/1,308/654 N
per row**; at 10 N, 8,000/4,000/2,000 N. Inertia, moments, creep and pressure variation add demand. Loaded comb
withdrawal needs friction bounds; unchanged terrain cannot be freely lifted.
Head mass/speed/isolation may fail. Torque needs actual gearing/lever arms.
Ideal lift work .04 F J/cell does not size motors: include head mass and `m*a`.

Power loss with comb seated should leave a positive keeper engaged. During a
transfer R needs mechanically retained grips and an arrested elevator; S needs
an arrested pickup. Neither mechanism exists yet. Mask readback alone cannot
prove jaw force, correct groove contact or supported departure. A detected
recoverable write error gets one row replay in the budget; stuck grips, lost
support, false readback or unknown height require a safe stop and do not carry
a <30-s recovery claim. R touches all 80 row cells even for one changed cell:
unchanged positions are invariant in the ideal replay, but loaded-neighbor
motion from clamp/frame deflection is unbounded. No coarse-row disturbance
exception has been approved.

For B=8/q=40/a=8,000, row allowance .25/.5/1/2 s gives
21.622/24.372/29.872/40.872 s; a 5×5 patch within one bank takes 13.393 s. Assumed
independent .1% failure per critical site gives all-good probabilities .00166,
2.74e-6 and 7.52e-12 for 6,400/12,800/25,600 sites; these are illustrative
replication penalties, **not predicted yields**. With unknown dependence, only
conditional bounds such as `P(any failure)<=min(1,N*p)` follow from a valid p.
One comb fault affects 80 sites, an eight-bank head fault affects 800 scheduled
cells, and reader bias can affect all 6,400. No simulation sample fraction is
used as reliability evidence. Roughness, first-layer effects, anisotropy,
shrinkage, warp, wear and creep remain missing physical evidence.

## Decision / next construction

Park S's four resident switching layers and missing selective pickup. Retain
R's **80 common releases instead of 6,400 independent resident locks** as a
useful architectural change, but park this 3-mm-stepped writer: hundreds of
setting lanes and unresolved grip forces do not earn local refinement. Do not
shrink its slots or increase acceleration again.

Next compare shared-stroke five-state media (binary decisions followed by common
multi-position motion) with commutated set/reset carriers. Build one finite
write/erase/read transition and complete arbitrary-map schedule, including
bit generation, retention, all clears, feed/return, registration and recovery.
Use this row-head budget and scalar shutter writing as controls. Stop if gains
recreate hundreds of unpriced axes, thousands of delicate resident locks or
E-084's inadequate setter-rate buffer. Shared support transfer remains a gate;
a better writer alone cannot promote R. No print or local compliance study yet.
