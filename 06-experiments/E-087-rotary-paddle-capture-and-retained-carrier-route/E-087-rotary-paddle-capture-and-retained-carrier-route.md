# Rotary paddles remove the fork flush but retain capture and completion gates

**Retain the stopped rotary paddle as a nominal section; reject its rigid
completion, the independently programmed long ternary paddle, and the tested
coplanar racetrack.** Rotation supplies a materially changed contact path,
without E-086's opposed jaws or extra parity-row flush. It does not yet supply
a manufactured, force-limited writer. A stationary setter with one returning
carrier is geometrically separable by bank, but its row-by-row return erases
much of the apparent transport advantage. No machine is selected or qualified.

Input main `63b3ca6`. Reproduce with
`python3 tools/curated-experiment-checks/E-087/rotary_carrier.py`.
Standard-library generated polygons, convex clipping, deterministic state
replay, interval corners and analytical itinerary/force calculations. All new
dimensions, errors, friction, accelerations, proof slots and budget reserves are
**explicit assumed scenarios**, not sourced X1C priors or measurements. No
random distribution, contact dynamics, FEA, production yield or fabrication
claim. Two coupling hypotheses and two transport routes are compared; numeric
refinements are not additional architectures.

## Short paddle, active rotation at a stopped row

The dog retains E-085's x stroke 1.2 mm, x width 0.8 mm and z=[0,1] mm.
Generate an actual rectangular paddle, length 3 mm and width 0.6 mm in x–z,
extruded 1.2 mm in y. Its pivot is (x,z)=(0.6,3) mm and its angle is measured
from downward vertical. An independent rotary data axis per column moves the
paddle; one common lift per bank raises pivots by 1.5 mm. An upward paddle at
180 degrees is bypass even when the common lift descends. This is **one rotary
axis plus a shared lift**, not independent vertical bypass actuators.

At the raised position, rotate from upward bypass to −35 degrees for set or
+35 for reset. Lower the common head; rotate toward the dog to −2.873196 degrees
for set or its reflection for reset; lift at that angle; rotate back to upward
bypass; prove clearance and index. Work comes from the rotary data drive.
Endpoint retention stores the command after contact separates. Independently
retained output supports and parked output cams remain boundary conditions;
the paddle does not support the tabletop. Supports, axle bearings, drives,
force limiter and retention are not generated by these section polygons.

All six old-state/set/reset/bypass paths and 256 two-by-two old/new maps replay
at 40/80/160 intervals per stage. Midstroke initial dogs are also exercised.
Finite polygon intersections check the target and both lateral neighbor states.
Nominal insertion has only **0.03418 mm** horizontal reserve. Axial separation
is 5.08−1.2=3.88 mm between paddle rows; no parity flush is required.

Active ±35-degree paddle envelopes have 1.14705-mm x separation. Programming
passes through much larger angles, so that active-envelope calculation alone
would be insufficient. All angles stay in [−35,180] degrees. Enclose the left
paddle in a disk of radius `sqrt(3²+0.3²)`. The right paddle's swept sector has
minimum distance at least `5.08*cos(35°)−0.3` from the left pivot. Their
**0.84633-mm** separation proves nominal paddle/paddle isolation for arbitrary
independent programming phases at equal common-lift height. It does not clear
the absent drive/shaft/frame assembly. Raised programming's minimum z is
`4.5−sqrt(3²+0.3²)=1.48504 mm`, above either dog.

### Capture and terminal uncertainty

Bound pivot x shift b, pivot z shift h, full blade-width error dw, and dog-stop
shift t separately by ±e. These are competing uncertainty boxes; a common
rail/batch can realize coherent adverse corners across 80 columns. No averaging
or probabilistic yield is justified. At the terminal angle theta, the finite
edge contacts the dog's upper corner and gives endpoint mismatch
`m=b+h*tan(theta)+dw/(2*cos(theta))−t`.
An independently derived straight-edge support equation agrees with clipping.

| e | Capture reserve, worst corner | Terminal mismatch interval |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0 mm | 0.03418 mm | 0 |
| 0.05 mm | −0.13135 mm | ±0.12754 mm |
| 0.10 mm | −0.29688 mm | ±0.25508 mm |
| 0.20 mm | −0.62794 mm | ±0.51016 mm |

Negative capture reserve means the programmed paddle reaches the dog during
lowering, before the intended rotary stroke. It is not a qualified capture
impact. Positive terminal mismatch is hard-stop interference; negative is
incomplete seating. A constant angular overdrive cannot remove both. Angular
backlash, boss width/height error, tilt, wear, contact indentation and pivot
bending are additional uncertainty, not unused margin. A changed approach angle
may improve capture, but cannot cure rigid completion. Stop this rigid drive
embodiment; investigate an actual force-limited completion/withdrawal joint
before further nominal speed optimization.

Lifting at the terminal angle nominally separates the contact without sweeping
back across the retained dog. A failed lift leaves **0.222707 mm²** intersection
with a zero dog at the next row. Index must be inhibited by actual per-paddle
clearance, not merely a common lift command. Capture energy, rotary recoil,
retention under vibration, guide friction and extraction of a jam remain open.

## Long retained paddle, common vertical energy

A materially different allocation stores one of three paddle angles on a
carrier: −15 degrees set, +15 reset, 180 bypass. A stationary setter programs
these angles; a common downward stroke supplies output work. This trades the
moving independent rotary drive for a retained angular state and shared energy.
It requires an angular latch/stops and a setter coupling; neither is granted
free or qualified by the nominal contact model.

Generate a 6×0.6-mm paddle about x=0.6 mm. Solving terminal contact puts the
pivot at z=1.412701 mm. The 1.2-mm lateral dog stroke requires **4.478461 mm**
vertical contact travel. Add 1.5 mm clearance before programming/transport,
for **5.978461 mm** lift and a raised pivot at z=7.391162 mm. Finite sections
replay both old states under set/reset and nominal lifting separation. Upward
bypass stays clear during the common stroke. The paddle extends to z=−4.46050
mm at its lowest pose and z=13.39866 mm during raised programming: **17.85916 mm** vertical
sweep, before bearings, rails or supports. Empty space below the dog is granted
for this early screen, not proved available.

**Independent same-plane programming fails.** In the bounded [−15,180]-degree
setter range, resetting a bypass paddle passes through about 114 degrees and
intersects a neighboring stored-bypass paddle by **0.393490 mm²**. The witness
persists at 80/160/320 angular subdivisions. Programming at the lowered pivot
also intersects an endpoint dog by 0.100 mm² when passing through zero angle.
An elevated setter clears dogs but does not clear neighboring retained paddles.
Reject this single-plane, independently programmed setter. Coordinated temporary
changes to neighbors, stacked setter planes or a folded/retracting blade are
changed mechanisms/schedules that could reopen it; this is not proof against
all rotary ternary memories or all paths through their joint configuration space.

For horizontal resistance F and Coulomb coefficient mu, a sliding wedge gives
`Fvertical/F=(tan(15°)+mu)/(1−mu*tan(15°))`, excluding pivot/guide friction and
inertia. With 80 simultaneous 0.3-N dogs and assumed mu=0.1/0.4, common force is
9.074/17.955 N. At zero friction the work identity is `Fvertical*dz=F*dx`.
Friction, retained-angle torque capacity, contact stress and rigid endpoint
errors still need a real joint. No force acceptance follows from this calculation.

## Carrier return and regional bank boundaries

The circulating reference has straight working/return runs joined by radius
6-mm semicircles in y–z. As a concrete routing rejection witness, put a **solid
rectangular carrier bar** 1.2 mm longitudinally by 3.6 mm vertically on the
centerline, rotating with its tangent. This is a declared bar geometry, not a
claim that an overlapping empty clearance envelope is a material collision.
It is deliberately smaller than the long-paddle assembly; its clearance would
not clear that assembly or its bearings/track.

At adjacent bank ends only one pitch apart, two facing turns have a shared
centerline point (y,z)=(2.54,0.564156) mm. Actual rotated bar polygons intersect
by **1.877303 mm²** there. A turning bar also intersects a parked neighboring
bottom-run bar by **1.52813 mm²**, providing a regional-operation witness.
Synchronized full operation does not prove that a local edit can leave its
neighbor parked. A bank of n rows has bar longitudinal span
`(n−1)*5.08+2*hypot(7.8,0.6)` mm. At n=10 that is **61.3661 mm**, exceeding its
50.8-mm bank pitch. Reject this coplanar radius/bar embodiment; staggered turns,
noncircular transitions and external return space need changed geometry.

A single **rectangular ferry route** per bank avoids those shared turns: the
stationary setter is above the first row; program while raised; translate above
dogs to target row j; lower/write/lift; return raised to the setter; reset and
read before the next row. Bar centers never leave that bank's first/last row
coordinates. Two 1.2-mm-deep bars retain **3.28 mm** longitudinal separation
under opposing ±0.20-mm rail offsets and full-depth errors ±0.20 mm. This
separates those finite bars and constant-y paddles only, not an unspecified
rail/drive/setter package. One carrier needs no separate passing lane; multiple
carriers need a different occupancy and handoff model.

The stopped head uses the same within-bank row corridor and can scan in either
direction. The ferry must revisit its fixed setter for every independent row
word. Buffering does not erase that preparation or E-084's sustained setter-rate
bound. Both keep output cams parked until complete mask and withdrawal proof;
local rewriting leaves independent column supports engaged. Reaction/vibration
of unchanged columns is not simulated.

## Decision and verification

Stop the rigid rotary drive, independent long-paddle setter and coplanar bar
loop as tested. Keep the short-paddle section as a changed nominal comparator:
its single station and bypass sweep remove known fork topology failures, while
capture and terminal force remain explicit reopening gates. The next useful
investigation is a **finite force-limited rotary completion and positive return
package**, with pivot/guide solids, capture during lowering, stopped-dog torque,
withdrawal/recoil and actual bypass scheduling. If it cannot survive bounded
errors/loads and the complete-channel allowance, retire it rather than perform
another nominal timing sweep. Carrier development requires changed setter and
turn geometry before richer contact simulation. No print, purchase or new agent.

Self-review only: clipping has analytic overlap/disjoint negative controls;
rotated rectangle area is invariant across 361 angles; edge support independently
agrees at 301 drive angles; exact error corners agree with the analytical
mismatch interval. Path refinements preserve endpoints and collision witnesses;
continuous nominal neighbor and raised-clearance bounds supplement sampling.
Positive-area collisions prove failure at the reported poses; an absence of
sampled collision alone would not certify a path. Rest-to-rest square-root
scaling, zero travel and frictionless work conservation are checked. No dynamic
time-step or mesh convergence is claimed because no such solver ran.
