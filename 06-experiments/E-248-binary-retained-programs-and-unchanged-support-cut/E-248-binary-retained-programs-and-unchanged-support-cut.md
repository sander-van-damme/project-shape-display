# Binary writing reduces traffic but adds support transactions

**Park this weighted-rack retained-target construction.** Three binary writes
replace E-243's five selective releases without a retained clutch per bit, but
positive holding between strokes makes it slower. Broad 6-mm codes need **10
heads / 800 channels / 27.579 s / $1,141 assumed bought allocation**, before
resolved routing. All 79 unchanged columns in a one-cell row update still receive
a support handoff. No pawl, keeper, rack, cam, guide or print development follows.

Input main `ae76bca`. Reproduce:
`python3 tools/curated-experiment-checks/E-248/screen.py` (stdlib; imports E-243;
stdout only). Evidence: **ideal replay, finite enumeration and bounded
calculations; self-review**. Hardware inputs are assumptions; no CAD, contact
simulation, sourced prices, process priors or physical measurements. Five heights
0/10/20/30/40 mm are a comparator, not a product resolution decision.

## Reconstruction and changed transaction

E-232 target stops solve retirement only after an admission oracle. Retracting
old supports before pickup drops elevated columns; retaining a rigid 2-mm tooth
in a 4-mm groove blocks 30-mm ascent. E-238 retains five-pulse programs but needs
ten codes, not nine deltas, because bounded paths depend on starting height. Its
broad double-buffer case retains five decodes/twenty contact stages at assumed
$588.60/27.842 s. E-243 head-only overrunning pawls remove admission writes, but
its six-state target setter still uses five physical masks ($815.80/28.842 s).
E-236 direct five pulses remain the simpler control: 400 channels/26.681 s,
unqualified lean allocation $503. E-247's nine displacement rails reduce loaded
transfers to one but broad unary writing needs eight passes and fails planar
packing. None supplies a selected machine.

The new hypothesis is **unloaded positional addition**, then E-243's retained
threshold control. Each head has 80 horizontal bars: codes 0…4 select target
height, code 5 is U. Bars have integer-pitch engagement positions. Common clamp C
locks them between operations. Moving teeth D engage selected bars; stationary
keepers K hold unselected bars while C opens. One retained mask drives two
complementary output routes, reused across bits; there are no separate bit leaves.

With terrain resident-supported, reset unloaded bars against an end stop. Old
program positions may be lost during reset because they are inactive. Guided
reset, completion sensing and terrain isolation are construction debts. For each
weight 1, 2, 4: write/read a mask with C seated; engage/prove D and complementary
K; open C; advance D by weight×pitch; seat/prove C; withdraw D/K; return empty.
Only then overwrite. D must capture bidirectionally. Clamp/read the final program
before head takeover. A q-channel group writer also owes group selection; the
model grants nonactive groups K support. All winning cases use q=80.

A flat common pusher fails after bit one: bar ends differ by p, so one plane
cannot contact both. Periodic rack engagement is the changed interface. Omitting
K leaves unselected bars unheld when C opens. Friction drag is a different,
unqualified retention scheme, not silently credited as positive support.

All 216 three-bar codes in six weight orders pass: **1,296 programs / 104,976
holding observations**. Positions remain within 0…5 pitches with C or D or K
active. Four negative controls reject missing K, missing D, live overwrite and
missing C reseat. Binary arithmetic and piecewise-linear motion independently
check final positions and intermediate bounds; contact/proof flags remain grants.

Composition with E-243 covers **15,625 three-cell maps / 4,031,250 support
observations**. Selected columns obey h=max(old,plane) on ascent and
h=max(target,plane) on descent; U is nominally fixed. Head pawls carry old heights,
overrun during pickup rise to 42 mm, retract only for selected cells at the apex,
and return before target seating. Resident comb seating precedes head withdrawal.
This is an ideal support witness, not contact equilibrium or gravity-following proof.

## Unchanged support: exact scope of the obstruction

| Transaction | Support during selected motion | Repeated consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Row comb → head pawls → comb | U pawl stays at old height | Two handoffs for every U in the row |
| E-236 crossed row/column paths with retained commands | U retains at least one resident path | Five row-comb withdrawal/reseat pairs; selected column bars also transfer other rows |
| Independently withdrawn rigid comb segments | U's original segment can remain engaged | Arbitrary masks require singleton segments: 80/row, 6,400/board |

If one rigid segment holds two columns, moving just one either releases its U
neighbor or is blocked. A partition into m independently released groups admits
2^m unions. Enumeration of all 15 four-cell partitions confirms that only four
singleton groups admit all 16 masks. This proves a **fixed rigid-group** result,
not a universal mechanism lower bound. Adaptive bridges, overrunning contacts or
selective column disengagement change the grammar and owe finite support and
replication accounts. Monolithic independent flexing teeth still repeat failure sites.

A 3×3 crossed-support enumeration checks 81 cell/contact states: every untouched
cell retains row OR column support. Neither fixed primary assignment can survive
all one-cell neighbor changes: a row-primary U loses that path during row access;
a column-primary U loses it during access elsewhere in its column. Redundancy
proves ideal support availability, not invariant contact loading.

One changed cell still causes **158 handoff events across 79 U cells** in the
new architecture. E-236 withdraws/reseats their row support five times and also
transfers other rows along selected column bars. These are interface events,
not measured displacement or equal probabilities of failure.

With actual bearing heights R/H, support is max(R,H) during overlap, then H when
R withdraws. Opposing ±e errors permit acquisition/release displacement up to
**2e=.30/.70/1.40 mm**, for e=.15/.35/.70. A common bias correlates the whole row;
even equal unloaded heights do not eliminate elastic load redistribution.
No actual stiffness or numerical product disturbance cap is established. Thus the
review's unchanged-row warning remains material; logical U is not physical isolation.

## Traffic, complete timing and recovery

Assume v=200 mm/s, a=8,000 mm/s², rest-to-rest t(d), J=t(2)+.003 s, D=5p,
n=80/q. Reset, clamp/read, five contact stages per bit, loaded/empty strokes,
group indexing and return are all inside the map boundary:

`W=2t(D)+2J+.010+n*sum[w=1,2,4](2t(wp)+5J)`
`  +(n-1)t(5.08)+t((n-1)*5.08)`.

Five stages: mask write, D/K approach, C withdraw, C insert, D/K clear. Concurrent
D/K approach is a favorable unconstructed grant. Total q80 writer travel is
24p versus unary 20p. At p6, **W=1.518587 s versus unary 1.121605 s**. Retained
E-243 service S=1.296283 s includes eight contact/mode stages, stopped ascent,
threshold-gated descent, bearing proofs, row feed and height reads. No program
rewrite during service, prepared media, buffer or overlap credit.

`T=2+(ceil(80/B)+1)(W+S)+t(42)+.010`.

Two common seconds allocate ingestion, initial registration/homing and final
settling. The extra row is one causal full retry after read/reset. Three expanded
timelines cross-check the equation. Sweep B1…80, all q divisors of 80,
p3/4/6/8 and lift a2,000/8,000; writer/contact/feed remain at a8,000.

Conditional schedules/allocations, not machines or market lower bounds. Resets
and clears are counted in time but are not selective decisions. The new writer
also sets 6,400 multilevel states. With one retried row: 19,440 decisions versus
32,400 for E-236/E-243. Compression is real; mechanical simplification is not.
B10/p6 no-retry time is 24.519 s. One/five contiguous visited rows in one bank:
7.875/19.134 s with retry, plus any seek beyond initial registration. Unvisited
rows stay on their combs; sparse visits owe transport; changed columns detour to 42.

Before takeover, a bad program is reset/rewritten with resident support present.
After a stable wrong target or on-grid partial result, seat/prove/read actual
heights, clear/reset, then reprogram. 125 wrong-target retries preserve a separate
U neighbor. A late corrupted U deliberately fails the unchanged-height control;
it is excluded from the recovery claim. Off-grid arrest, stuck pawl, broken tooth
or false proof requires supported shutdown/service, not promised <30-s repair.
Power loss during writing leaves terrain on its comb; service requires retained
outputs and bank-local elevator arrest. Next-tooth catching after up to 10-mm
fall is not qualified arrest. Writer-only throw/proof sensitivity at B10/p6 spans
26.162 s (1 mm/.003 s) to 30.654 s (4 mm/.010 s), not a license to shrink contacts.

## Decision

Binary addition earns its traffic reduction but pays for holding during each
weighted return. It does not remove the loaded-support cut. Rigid segmentation
restores thousands of releases; crossed support preserves availability, not an
invariant primary contact. No materially simpler complete path survives the
screen. Preserve source/counterexamples and stop this writer before refinement.

Next discriminator: a **changed column-to-support interface** allowing a selected
column to pass a stationary positive support while U stays bearing on it, with
selection/return confined to a shared head. First reconstruct prior bayonet/lane
failures, then test finite crossing and top/guide motion. Charge rotation, lateral
translation, decoupled caps, joints and returns at 6,400-fold scale. Reject renamed
local latches or a hidden withdrawal of U supports. This hypothesis does not reopen
the parked ratchet or micrometre-clearance families.
