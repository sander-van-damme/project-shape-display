# Target stops remove retirement writes, not old-height support

**Park these fixed-mask constructions before stop/latch development.** With
E-231's rigid periodic shelves, releasing all changed columns before pickup
leaves raised columns unsupported; retaining those supports blocks ascent.
Prewritten targets do not provide old-height release. The screened stop memories
also add substantial programming, load-contact and routing burdens. No CAD or print.

Input main `063fa 01`. Reproduce:
`python 3 tools/curated-experiment-checks/E-232/screen.py` (stdlib, stdout only).
Evidence: **architecture hypotheses, ideal replay and bounded calculations;
self-review**. No measurements, sourced prices, calibrated X 1C distributions,
contact simulation or constructed routing. Non-product numbers are assumptions.
Five 10-mm levels are a comparator, not a product resolution decision.

## Acquisition fails before target-stop refinement

Consider `(10, 30, 20)→(20, 0, 20)` mm. A fixed mask selects the first two columns;
the third stays on its head column bar. Withdraw the row comb for selected motion.

- Retracting changed-cell bars at setup leaves the first two columns above the
  zero-height pickup with no support. A plane brake cannot arrest them.
- Keeping the bars inserted causes groove-roof interference. A 4-mm groove and
  2-mm tooth permit **at most 2 mm** upward motion from the favorable seated
  face; the two columns require 30/10 mm. This fails at zero process error.
- E-231 releases each old support only after pickup at that height. Retaining
  this solution leaves **five live ascent writes**. Automatic bottom trips owe
  80 retained trip/output assemblies/head or 6, 400 resident devices, including
  overlap/reset. A second old-height support register restores duplicated load
  paths or independently held acquisition axes. Neither is free passive pickup.

Target stops already deployed before pickup also collide for old=10,target=30.
A corrected **conditional** order programs stops 42 mm below final position,
raises selected columns to 42, raises the whole stop carrier 42, descends the
pickup, seats/proves the row comb, then lowers empty stops. Two-mm overtravel
clears target 40 during deployment and increases detour. Unchanged-cell stop
noses must retract with the fixed mask or use their old heights; the latter
still makes within-row contact and owes a disturbance check.

The executable grants an explicit **admission oracle**: individually release old
support on pickup contact. Then `h=max(plane,target)` on descent passes 15, 625
three-cell map pairs. This verifies only ideal retirement, not fixed-mask ascent.
Two negative tests preserve the unsupported and blocked-ascent witnesses.

## Routing and functional-error envelope

E-231's 80-mm column has bottom h−80; resident supports 0/−10 and guides 0/−20.
Pickup reaches −38 at h 42. Parked stop top is target−122, at most −82, below the
lowest foot by 2. Deployment adds 42. Bases/beds/spines lie lower still. Pickup and
stop need separate bearing lanes throughout overlap; `max()` supplies no lanes.

For combined lateral error e=.15/.35/.70 mm, a 4-mm foot split into two 2-mm
lanes with centered 1-mm noses leaves `.5−e`=.35/.15/−.20 mm, before separator
or bending. E-231's 1-mm side blade between 4-mm stems at 5.08 pitch retains
`.04−e`<0 throughout. These are labelled bounds, not X 1C distributions.

Stair indexing is less sensitive: 8-mm land/2-mm nose gives `3−2e`=2.70/2.30/1.60
mm margin for opposing writer/land errors. But its 40-mm block translated 32
occupies a **72-mm feed-axis union**. Eight banks have 50.8-mm spacing; only B≤5
fit this simple same-plane allocation before frames. Depth staggering/remote
writers owe new paths and strokes. Direct stairs also span neighboring rows
while rising; no collision-free carrier or narrow routed follower is constructed.

Parking/overtravel `2−2e`=1.70/1.30/.60 mm; opposing row/stop errors permit
`2e`=.30/.70/1.40-mm takeover motion. Frame warp, shaft twist, print bias and joined
406.4-mm beams correlate rows/banks. The 256-mm bed requires modules. Creep,
buckling, roughness, stack seating, layer strength and gravity following remain
unqualified; no independent-cell yield or universal tolerance is asserted.

A changed admission principle could use **height-layer row supports and unique
feet**. A finite witness must still be escaped: lower foot thickness f hits an
upper shelf thickness t at `plane=upper_level−f−t`, before pickup reaches the
upper foot. Early withdrawal drops the upper column. Nine f/t cases (1/2/4 mm)
fail this simple sequence. A shared supported ramp/changed approach needs a
new transaction; zero-thickness layers do not establish an escape.

## Support proof and replication

downstream transmission failure. Mask/memory locks must retain without power.
Descent transfers pickup→stop at overlap; never withdraw loaded stops before
actual row-seat proof. Read 80 old heights, mask outputs, stop heights and final
heights/row, plus locks, plane/carrier position and handoffs. Flags cannot prove
load bearing. Optical paths are unbuilt; .010-s reads are grants. At **assumed**

## Redirect

Stops can replace five retirement writes **if** supported acquisition exists;
contact with a rising plane cannot erase all ten writes. Preserve this causal
separation and stop the bars/stairs/stacks/hybrid as tested. Do not repair them
through another pawl, thin blade, loaded axis or travelling cassette.

Next screen a genuinely shared **old-support release transaction**: height-layer
row supports on unique feet, including finite-thickness supported crossing,
compared with retracting periodic combs. First prove that higher old columns
remain supported as lower feet cross. Reject zero-thickness cartoons and escapes
needing independent old-height axes, duplicated grips or 6, 400 local trips. Only
a surviving support path earns further target-memory scheduling or geometry.
