# Common-datum particle-stack height memory

A column follower rests on a counted stack of spheres in a vertical tube. The
number of particles stores height; compressive contacts and a grounded bottom
fork support the load. A travelling row of feeders docks at the **fixed bottom
of every stack**, avoiding acquisition of a foot at an unknown height. This is
solid-length storage, distinct from trapped fluid, tensile winding and the
binary moving structural layers. The follower needs guidance and a downward
return bias; particle removal alone cannot pull a jammed follower down.

Proposed transaction: a pocketed pusher supports the stack, unloads the fork,
transfers one particle in/out, returns the grounded fork, seats and proves support,
then repeats or undocks. Two support levels are needed during exchange; the fork
cannot simply withdraw beneath a loaded stack. Shared powered feed strokes plus
individually selected head couplings provide addressing. Unchanged forks remain
engaged. The transfer, return, actuator arrest and retained selection are open
mechanisms, not supplied by the existence of beads or a clearance section.

Count pulses are insufficient readback: use an underside follower-height mark
and support-proof step before undocking. A missed/doubled particle, bridging or
false acceptance leaves a wrong or unsupported cell. Stop with the head supported,
retain selection, reverse at most one transfer after readback, then isolate and
service the tube. A common reader/feed fault may affect an entire bank. Shared
reservoir and recirculation must work on repeated arbitrary maps, including an
all-high map; spare storage cannot be omitted.

E-126 rejects the bounded **four-rest-to-rest-stroke feeder** on throughput,
including 320 heads. It does not reject continuous pocketed feeding. The tested
4-mm sphere scenarios need 11 particles per site to guarantee geometric reach
above 40 mm before elastic compression: 70,400 particles, 6,400 slotted tubes and
forks, return elements, plus feeders, selectors, sensors and a circulation path.
E-130 tests a level translating pallet and a circular pocket path at two retained
heights in both directions. Grounded endpoint seats admit sphere-contact paths,
but intermediate input removal still needs linear restraint or rotary arrest;
inline repetition also intersects an unchanged upstream stack. Captivation alone
does not ground the moving support. Stop these embodiments, not every solid-length
mechanism. Reopen only with a changed grounded-support/arrest path and explicit
feed routing that escapes both these failures and the serial-cycle bound.
No print, purchase or reliability claim.
