# Electroadhesive selection with separate service support

**Bounded architecture hypothesis, not demonstrated hardware.** Descriptors:
active-matrix electrical addressing; electrode charge as transient selector
memory; shared motor elevator; toothed ground pawl as height memory/load path;
prismatic columns; two switchable friction interfaces plus one pawl per cell.
This changes selection and power coupling from independent rack heads.

Two thin electroadhesive ribbons at each site couple (1) its long column tail
to a vertically translating elevator and (2) its pawl-release link to a shared
lateral release bar. An active switch and stored command at each ribbon avoid
passive row/column half-voltage holding. Row data are written before shared
motion; uncommanded release links stay disconnected. Release spring/cam resets
the link after the pulse. The elevator takes load and slightly unloads the
pawl before withdrawal. Captured changed cells descend to zero, then rise to
their requested teeth, where the release ribbon disengages, the spring inserts
the pawl and support transfers before the moving ribbon releases. Unchanged
cells retain grounded pawls throughout. Five 10-mm-spaced levels are a research
workload; 40-mm usable stroke is not generated by the electrode.

Long tails provide 80 mm of contact track for all relative old-height/elevator
positions; a 20-mm contact needs roughly 100 mm usable tail length. A 2-mm-wide
foil face nominally fits 5.08-mm pitch, but pawl/guide/insulation packing is
unverified. A common mechanical service clamp may back up all grounded pawls,
but it must remain closed on unrelated cells; a global unlock is disallowed.
The pawl already provides power-off service support. A sheet clamp alone cannot
replace local height memory without an independently held unchanged subset.

Two 6,400-site electrode/switch/storage arrays, conductive tracks, 6,400
pawls/returns and guides repeat; HV supply, motors, controller and underside
line reader are shared. Tail marks read before/after transfer plus elevator
force change and a small proof unload detect slip or failed pawl engagement.
On failure keep the moving grip powered and stop the module; engage an
accessible mechanical service catch before power removal or replacement.
Unverified adhesion is not a safe fallback. Common dielectric contamination,
charge decay, residual adhesion and scanner bias can defeat many sites.

E-063's ideal 40-mm² friction model gives 0.00105–1.99 N over bounded gaps,
films and voltage. Only 12/36 cases reach a 0.05-N release-link scenario; one
reaches 1 N, none 10 N. Therefore retain **low-force selection**, not direct
10-N electrostatic service hold. The moving grip still must carry actual
transient load: replace it with a mechanically wedged collet if the force
bound fails, retaining the electrode only as its command clutch. That mutation
adds a collet/reset interface and must be costed; it is not a free rescue.
With $250 shared reserve, $500 leaves $0.0391/site for *both* channels and all
bought local material. No sourced HV-array BOM establishes this. Next question:
can the two command clutches switch an unloaded positive collet/pawl within
that budget and pitch, including discharge and proof? Stop before CAD/printing
until that route exists. Electronics/foil assembly is outside demonstrated PLA
fabrication, although the frame and guides remain printed.
