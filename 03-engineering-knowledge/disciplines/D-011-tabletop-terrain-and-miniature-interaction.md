# Tabletop terrain and miniature interaction

Sources checked 2026-10-12. The [official rules](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/br-2024/playing-the-game)
use 5-foot squares and one square for Small/Medium creatures, distinguish difficult
terrain from cover, and forbid diagonal movement through a wall corner. **Creature
space is not a specified miniature-base diameter.** Neither those rules nor the
sources below establish 5.08-mm pins,40-mm relief,30-second tolerable waits or $1000
willingness to pay. Those remain inherited engineering/value hypotheses.

Mike Shea's [2016 survey](https://slyflourish.com/2016_dm_survey_results.html)
reports 6,600 responses:63% preferred gridded combat,19% abstract maps,18% theater of
the mind. It recruited online, explicitly acknowledges sample bias, and is historical
self-report—not a current population estimate, observed session sample or demand for
physical terrain. It supports retaining clear grid positioning while accommodating
other play styles, not maximizing mechanical detail.

His [in-person map practice](https://slyflourish.com/drawing_maps.html) includes flat
miniature surfaces, hiding prepared maps with paper/cloth, and using encounter-scale
windows within larger dungeons. His [terrain comparison](https://slyflourish.com/battle_map_comparisons.html)
describes setup, teardown and flexibility costs of elaborate terrain; it is one
experienced GM's assessment, not a measured acceptable-wait distribution. Our inference:
assess total preparation and interruptions, including player choices that bypass a
prepared scene. A display that takes less time to actuate but more time to prepare
or repair may lose to a drawn mat.

The contextual sample now includes these distinct play functions. Only the original
three windows are numerically rasterized; the last two are **qualitative coverage
checks**, not extra timing observations or a statistically representative corpus.

**Representation rule proposed for evaluation:** require a declared movement,
cover, elevation or support function for raised geometry. Keep solid walls, stairs,
platforms and significant ground relief. Use a visible footprint/prop for relevant
trees and furnishings, visual markings for texture, loose rubble and optional water.
State the DM's depth/cover rulings separately: a 20-mm ravine is not 20 mm of fictional
distance. Miniatures must fit the intended route and resting spots; thin wall
accuracy is secondary to those affordances. These are engineering inferences from
practice and rules; enjoyment and immersion still need owner/player evaluation.

**Reveal workflow:** precompute known terrain before play; keep unchanged loaded pins
supported during local changes. Hidden relief can disclose rooms even when its color
is hidden. Paper/cloth over an uneven board can snag or reveal contours; projection
does not hide the shape. A visual-only reveal can avoid mechanical motion only if
its concealment already works. Unknown rooms need paid motion, plus removal/replacement
of miniatures or props in the changed region. Never blindly home a populated board.
The useful comparison is one encounter setup plus several local reveals and recovery,
not only an empty-board map switch. No observed frequency or zero-time prop flow is assumed.
