Tileable mechanical metamaterials can store discrete state in the geometry of their unit cells and change structural response after reprogramming.

This is relevant because a dense printed layer could act as reusable mechanical memory or selection logic without one purchased latch per terrain column. The most useful transfer is likely a **small-stroke memory layer that controls a separate 40 mm lift**, not a metamaterial cell that directly becomes the visible terrain column.

Relevant precedent: Chen, Pauly & Reis demonstrated independently writable, bistable mechanical "m-bits" with distinct write and read phases in a tiled array ([Nature 589, 386–390, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-020-03123-5)).

Main project risks are printable pitch, state cross-talk, switching-force spread, creep/fatigue and the cost/throughput of writing thousands of cells.
