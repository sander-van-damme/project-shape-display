**Core principle:** An array of independently switchable multistable unit cells stores mechanical
state in the structure itself. Writing and reading can be separate operations.

**Why it may matter here:** A compact memory/selector layer could encode terrain decisions while a different
mechanism provides the long 40 mm stroke and tabletop load support.

**What it does not solve:** It does not automatically provide scalable addressing, long travel or high load
capacity. A 6,400-cell array still needs a fast writer and low cross-talk.

**Cheapest discriminating experiment:** Print a small final-pitch array and measure independent write force, state
retention, neighbor disturbance, cycle life and print-to-print variation. Treat
it as a memory layer, not as the terrain column itself.

**Precedent:** Chen, Pauly & Reis, *A reprogrammable mechanical metamaterial with stable
memory*, Nature 589, 386–390 (2021):
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-020-03123-5
